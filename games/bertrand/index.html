What the two games mean

Bertrand as seen in class
Each round Airbus sees your price and answers with its best response. Follow the arrows in the best-response chart: your price, Airbus’s answer, your next price, and so on, as the prices walk toward the Bertrand–Nash equilibrium. Once your price is between the Nash price and a few dollars above it, Airbus settles on its Nash price for the rest of the game, and your best answer is the Nash price too.
Collusion cartel
Airbus matches whatever price you set, as if the two firms had agreed to charge the same price. With identical firms, the price that maximizes your profit then also maximizes the two firms’ joint profit.

Note: this is a simplification that follows the Bertrand model from class. Because Airbus sees your price before it answers, the rounds are really played in turns. If the firms took that into account, the prices would end up a bit different (slightly above the Nash price).
