The setup

Each firm’s demand falls with its own price and rises with its rival’s price (the planes are substitutes):

\[ Q_A = a_A - b_A P_A + c_A P_B, \qquad Q_B = a_B - b_B P_B + c_B P_A \]

Each firm earns its markup on every plane it sells:

\[ \pi_A = (P_A - MC_A)\,Q_A, \qquad \pi_B = (P_B - MC_B)\,Q_B \]

The game’s default numbers are the same for both firms:

so \(Q_A = 400 - 2P_A + P_B\) and \(Q_B = 400 - 2P_B + P_A\).

Quantities cannot be negative. If Boeing’s price is so high that it sells nothing, the game stops letting Boeing’s price push customers to Airbus (the “choke price” in the game). None of the answers below come close to it, so you can ignore it when solving.

Step 1 for every game: the best-response function

Take Airbus’s price \(P_A\) as given. Boeing chooses \(P_B\) to maximize

\[ \pi_B = (P_B - MC_B)\,(a_B - b_B P_B + c_B P_A). \]

Set the derivative with respect to \(P_B\) to zero

\[ \frac{\partial \pi_B}{\partial P_B} = a_B - 2\,b_B P_B + c_B P_A + b_B\,MC_B = 0 \]

Solve for \(P_B\)

\[ P_B^{BR}(P_A) = \frac{a_B + b_B\,MC_B + c_B\,P_A}{2\,b_B} \]

Airbus’s best response is the same with the letters swapped:

\[ P_A^{BR}(P_B) = \frac{a_A + b_A\,MC_A + c_A\,P_B}{2\,b_A} \]

Both slope upward: when your rival raises its price, your best price rises too, but by less (prices are strategic complements).

Shortcut. The first-order condition can be rewritten as \(Q_B = b_B\,(P_B - MC_B)\). So whenever a firm is on its best response, its profit is \(\pi = b\,(P - MC)^2\).

Check your answer (game’s default numbers)
\[ P_B^{BR}(P_A) = \frac{400 + 2 \cdot 232 + P_A}{4} = 216 + \tfrac14\,P_A, \qquad P_A^{BR}(P_B) = 216 + \tfrac14\,P_B \]

Simultaneous Bertrand–Nash equilibrium

Both firms choose at the same time, so in equilibrium each price must be a best response to the other. Solve the two best-response functions together.

With identical firms, \(P_A = P_B = P^*\)

\[ P^* = \frac{a + b\,MC + c\,P^*}{2b} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P^* = \frac{a + b\,MC}{2b - c} \]

Quantity and profit (from the shortcut)

\[ Q^* = b\,(P^* - MC), \qquad \pi^* = b\,(P^* - MC)^2 \]

With different firms, solving the two equations gives

\[ P_A^* = \frac{2b_B\,(a_A + b_A MC_A) + c_A\,(a_B + b_B MC_B)}{4\,b_A b_B - c_A c_B}, \qquad P_B^* = \frac{2b_A\,(a_B + b_B MC_B) + c_B\,(a_A + b_A MC_A)}{4\,b_A b_B - c_A c_B} \]

Why firms end up at \(P^*\). Suppose the firms take turns, each replying to the other’s current price with its best response. Since \(P^* = P^{BR}(P^*)\), each reply satisfies \(P^{BR}(P_{\text{rival}}) - P^* = \frac{c}{2b}\,(P_{\text{rival}} - P^*)\): it shrinks the gap to \(P^*\) by the factor \(\frac{c}{2b}\), so prices converge to \(P^*\) whenever \(c < 2b\) (with different firms, whenever \(4\,b_A b_B > c_A c_B\)). This needs both firms to reply only to today’s price. A firm that knows its rival will answer does better by holding its price, and that is the Leader game.

Check your answer (game’s default numbers)
\[ P^* = 216 + \tfrac14 P^* \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac34 P^* = 216 \;\Rightarrow\; P^* = 288 \] \[ Q^* = 2\,(288 - 232) = 112, \qquad \pi^* = 56 \times 112 = 6{,}272 \ \text{for each firm} \]

Taking turns from the cartel price, each reply closes three quarters of the gap: \(316 \to 295 \to 289.75 \to 288.44 \to \cdots \to 288\).

In the game, Airbus cannot see your price, so it charges its equilibrium price in every round. Check that your profit is highest when you charge that same price: each price is then a best response to the other, which is what makes it an equilibrium.

Leader Stackelberg: you move first

You set your price first, and Airbus sees it before replying with its best response. So choose \(P_B\) knowing that Airbus will answer with \(P_A = P_A^{BR}(P_B)\).

1. Write Airbus’s reaction as a line

\[ P_A = \alpha + \beta\,P_B, \qquad \alpha = \frac{a_A + b_A\,MC_A}{2\,b_A}, \quad \beta = \frac{c_A}{2\,b_A} \]

2. Substitute it into your demand

\[ Q_B = a_B - b_B P_B + c_B(\alpha + \beta P_B) = \underbrace{(a_B + c_B\,\alpha)}_{\tilde a} \;-\; \underbrace{(b_B - c_B\,\beta)}_{\tilde b}\,P_B \]

3. Maximize \((P_B - MC_B)(\tilde a - \tilde b\,P_B)\), like a monopolist facing this demand

\[ P_B^{L} = \frac{\tilde a + \tilde b\,MC_B}{2\,\tilde b} \]

4. Airbus’s price follows from its reaction

\[ P_A = \alpha + \beta\,P_B^{L} \]
Check your answer (game’s default numbers)
\[ Q_B = 400 - 2P_B + \left(216 + \tfrac14 P_B\right) = 616 - \tfrac74\,P_B \] \[ P_B^{L} = \frac{616 + \tfrac74 \cdot 232}{2 \cdot \tfrac74} = \frac{616 + 406}{3.5} = 292, \qquad P_A = 216 + \tfrac{292}{4} = 289 \] \[ Q_B = 616 - \tfrac74 \cdot 292 = 105, \qquad Q_A = 400 - 2 \cdot 289 + 292 = 114 \] \[ \pi_B = (292 - 232) \times 105 = 6{,}300, \qquad \pi_A = (289 - 232) \times 114 = 6{,}498 \]

The follower (Airbus) earns more than the leader: in price competition, moving second is an advantage. In the game, Boeing sells nothing above 352, the price where \(616 - \tfrac74 P_B\) reaches zero.

Follower Stackelberg: Airbus moves first

Now Airbus leads: it picks its price anticipating your best response, and you reply once you see it. Airbus solves the Leader problem above with the roles swapped; your job is Step 1: find your best response.

1. Airbus’s price: substitute your best response \(P_B = \alpha_B + \beta_B P_A\) into Airbus’s demand and maximize

\[ P_A^{L} = \frac{\tilde a_A + \tilde b_A\,MC_A}{2\,\tilde b_A}, \qquad \tilde a_A = a_A + c_A\,\alpha_B, \quad \tilde b_A = b_A - c_A\,\beta_B \]

2. Your price: your best response to Airbus’s price

\[ P_B = P_B^{BR}\!\left(P_A^{L}\right) \]
Check your answer (game’s default numbers)

By symmetry Airbus leads with \(P_A = 292\). Your best response:

\[ P_B = 216 + \tfrac{292}{4} = 289 \] \[ Q_B = 400 - 2 \cdot 289 + 292 = 114, \qquad \pi_B = 57 \times 114 = 6{,}498 \] \[ Q_A = 105, \qquad \pi_A = 60 \times 105 = 6{,}300 \]

You are on your best response, so the shortcut works too: \(\pi_B = 2\,(289 - 232)^2 = 6{,}498\).

Collusion cartel

The firms agree on prices that maximize their joint profit \(\pi_A + \pi_B\). With identical firms they charge the same price \(P\), and each sells \(Q = a - (b - c)\,P\):

\[ \max_{P}\; 2\,(P - MC)\,\bigl(a - (b - c)\,P\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P^{C} = \frac{a + (b - c)\,MC}{2\,(b - c)} \]

In the game, Airbus matches your price, so your own profit \((P - MC)\,\bigl(a - (b - c)P\bigr)\) is maximized at the same price.

Check your answer (game’s default numbers)
\[ P^{C} = \frac{400 + 232}{2} = 316, \qquad Q = 400 - 316 = 84, \qquad \pi = 84 \times 84 = 7{,}056 \ \text{each} \]

That beats the Nash profit of 6,272. But the cartel is fragile: Airbus’s best response to 316 is \(216 + \tfrac{316}{4} = 295\), which would earn it \(63 \times 126 = 7{,}938\). Each firm is tempted to undercut.
